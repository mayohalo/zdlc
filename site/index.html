Discreet Log Contracts on Zcash

Native programmability for Zcash, shielded end to end.

zDLC makes ZEC programmable: escrows, hedges, payroll, loans and bets that settle on real-world outcomes, on Zcash itself. Two people lock ZEC together, sign a payout for every outcome in advance, and an oracle's signature decides which one becomes valid. Money goes in from the shielded pool and comes back out to it.

Where it comes from

An MIT design, proven on Bitcoin, brought to Zcash

2017

Discreet Log Contracts

Tadge Dryja, at MIT's Digital Currency Initiative, publishes the design: oracle contracts that look like ordinary multisig on chain, with an oracle that never learns about them.

2019–

Built out on Bitcoin

Adaptor signatures replace the original key tweaks; the dlcspecs standardize the messages; libraries such as rust-dlc and DLC Dev Kit make them buildable.

Now

zDLC on Zcash

The same oracle messages (compatible with dlcspecs oracles), on Zcash v6 transactions, with funding and payouts that go through the shielded pool.

The idea

Contracts without contract code

There is no VM to run contract logic. The logic is agreed and signed before any money moves; the chain only sees a two-party output and, at the end, one spend of it.

Logic
Pre-signed transactions held by the two parties, not code run by every node
Custody
A 2-of-2 between the parties; no contract address, no custodian
Oracle
Signs a public statement and never learns who relies on it
Settlement
One ordinary transaction, usually into shielded notes; a payout or time-locked refund if someone walks away

How it works

Every payout is signed. Only one ever unlocks.

What the chain seesOne multisig output, indistinguishable from any other.
1 · Announce

The oracle commits

It publishes a one-time nonce for the event. From that and its key, every outcome has a point anyone can compute.

2 · Pre-sign

Both sides sign every payout

Each payout is signed with an adaptor signature locked to its outcome's point, and a refund is locked to a height.

3 · Fund

Only then, the money

The funding goes into a 2-of-2 last, so nobody commits funds before holding every payout.

4 · Settle

One payout becomes valid

The oracle's signature completes exactly one. Most contracts skip it and close cooperatively to shielded addresses.

Privacy

Discreet by design, shielded by Zcash

A DLC already keeps the oracle out of the contract. Zcash adds what Bitcoin cannot: the money comes from the shielded pool and goes back to it. Here is who learns what.

WhoLearnsNever learns
The oracleThe event it is asked to attest, and the outcome it signsWho uses the attestation, how much is at stake, or any payout
Your counterpartyThe terms you agreed and what it is owedYour other funds: a shielded deposit is redacted before it sees the funding Zcash
The deskThat two identities exchanged messages, their sizes and timesAny message: each is signed by the sender and sealed to the recipient
A chain observerA 2-of-2 output with its amountWhere the money came from or went, on a deposit and a cooperative close Zcash
You, restored from your seedEvery contract you were in, from encrypted memos only that seed can read ZcashNothing is lost with the device

Shielded in

Fund a contract by scanning a payment link with any Zcash wallet. The deposit goes to a receiver only that contract uses, so it ties to nothing else you own.

Shielded out

A cooperative close pays every party, and up to 16 payees, straight into shielded notes in one transaction: amounts and recipients hidden.

What stays visible

The 2-of-2 and its amount while a contract is open. If a contract settles by a pre-signed payout instead of a close, that payout is transparent until moved into the shielded pool.

What to build

Anything two parties can agree on and an oracle can attest

An oracle can sign a price, a ruling, or a fact it reads from the chain. The ones marked in gold ship as templates today: plain inputs in, exact terms out, checked byte for byte by the side that accepts.

Why DLC is better on Zcash

Bitcoin hides the contract. Zcash also hides the money.

On Bitcoin, a DLC's funding traces back to your coins and its payouts land on addresses anyone can follow. Zcash adds a shielded pool, encrypted memos and viewing keys, and each one changes what a contract can do.

Shielded in and out

Fund from shielded notes and close straight back into them. The contract's money has no visible past and no visible future; only the 2-of-2 in between is public.

Pay from any wallet

Unified addresses and ZIP 321 links let anyone fund a contract by scanning a QR code, shielded, with a memo naming the contract.

Encrypted memos

Every shielded note carries a private message. zDLC uses it for recovery records only your seed can read, and for private quotes delivered straight to one counterparty.

Viewing keys

An oracle can attest that a shielded payment arrived by reading it with a viewing key: proof of payment without making the payment public and without holding a spending key.

Payouts that stay private

A cooperative close pays every party, and up to 16 payees, in one shielded transaction: who was paid and how much stays off the public record.

A private desk

Quotes and contract messages travel sealed between the two parties; a quote can arrive as a shielded memo with nothing posted publicly.

Also built in, as on any good DLC stack: settlement on the agreement of two oracles, a keyless watcher for the refund, and contracts that stay valid across a network upgrade.

Developers

One object runs the contract

Describe a contract with a template and react to events. The kit runs setup, funding, the close, the fallbacks and recovery.

  • Rustzdlc-kit, with swappable transport, storage, oracles and chain
  • Any languagezdlc-node with a REST API and OpenAPI spec; zdlc-cli
  • TypeScript@zdlc/client; @zdlc/sdk embeds a node in Node.js or runs the protocol in the browser
  • Local stackjust dev: a chain, a desk, two oracles and two funded nodes
import { ZdlcClient, templates } from "@zdlc/client";

const z = new ZdlcClient("http://127.0.0.1:7950", { token });

const { id } = await z.offer(client, templates.escrow({
  amount: "1.5",
  question: "Milestone 1 delivered?",
}));

for await (const ev of z.stream()) {
  if (ev.kind === "awaitingDeposit") showQr(ev.uri);
  if (ev.kind === "settled") console.log(ev.how, ev.txid);
}
zdlc-cli offer escrow --to $CLIENT --amount 1.5 \
  --question "Milestone 1 delivered?"

zdlc-cli --url $CLIENT_NODE accept $ID --funding deposit
zdlc-cli --url $CLIENT_NODE qr $ID   # the ZIP 321 link and QR
zdlc-cli events --follow            # offered › funded › attested › settled
import init, * as core from "@zdlc/sdk/core";  // WebAssembly
await init();

const terms = core.templateTerms(template, announcements, tip + 1);
const offer = core.offer(ctx, terms);                // → your peer
const { sign } = core.sign(ctx, offer, accept, tip);  // → your peer
const { tx } = core.cet(ctx, "offerer", offer, accept, sign,
                       announcements, attestations, tip);
