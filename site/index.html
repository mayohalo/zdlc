zDLC

Contracts without contract code

Discreet by design. Enforced by signatures.

Private, oracle-settled payments on Zcash.

Two parties lock funds and pre-sign every possible payout with adaptor signatures; an oracle's signature makes exactly one of them valid. It all runs on Zcash as it is, with no protocol change. Money comes in from the shielded pool and goes back out to it; while a contract is open, only its collateral is visible on chain, never its terms.

Where it comes from

An MIT design, proven on Bitcoin, brought to Zcash

2017

Discreet Log Contracts

Tadge Dryja, at MIT's Digital Currency Initiative, publishes the design: oracle contracts that look like ordinary multisig on chain, with an oracle that never learns about them.

2019–

Built out on Bitcoin

Adaptor signatures replace the original key tweaks; the dlcspecs standardize the messages; libraries such as rust-dlc and DLC Dev Kit make them buildable.

Now

zDLC on Zcash

The same oracle messages (compatible with dlcspecs oracles), on Zcash v6 transactions, with funding and payouts that go through the shielded pool.

What the chain seesOne multisig output and its amount.
1 · Announce

The oracles commit

Each publishes a one-time nonce for the event. From it and the oracle's key, every outcome has a point anyone can compute.

2 · Pre-sign

Both sides sign every payout

Each payout carries an adaptor signature locked to its outcome's point, and a refund is locked to a height.

3 · Fund

Only then, the money

The funding is signed last, so nobody commits funds before holding every payout and the refund.

4 · Settle

One payout becomes valid

The oracles' signatures complete exactly one. Usually the parties skip it and close cooperatively into shielded notes.

The math

s·G = R + e·P

P, R
an oracle's public key and the nonce it announced
e
the hash of R, P and one possible outcome
s
the oracle's signature on the outcome that happens

Why it is called a "discreet log" contract

Before the event, anyone can compute the right side for every outcome. When the oracle signs, its signature s is the discrete logarithm of exactly one of those points, and it completes the payout both parties pre-signed to that point. The oracle publishes a statement; it never learns which contracts use it.

With two oracles, each payout is locked to the sum of their two points for that outcome. Only the sum of both signatures completes it, so one oracle alone unlocks nothing, and two that disagree unlock nothing: the refund settles it.

Why Zcash

DLC hides the contract. Zcash shields money in and out.

On Bitcoin, a DLC's funding traces back to your coins and its payouts land on addresses anyone can follow. On Zcash, money enters from the shielded pool and leaves to it. The temporary 2-of-2 in between stays public, and can still link a funding and its settlement to each other, but not to the people behind them.

Pay from any wallet

Unified addresses and ZIP 321 links let anyone fund a contract by scanning a QR code, shielded, with a memo naming the contract.

Encrypted memos

Each contract leaves a recovery record in a memo only your seed can read, and a maker can send one taker a private quote with nothing posted publicly.

Viewing keys

For proof-of-payment contracts, the oracle receives the incoming viewing key of an address used only for that repayment. It can read payments arriving there, and could disclose them, but cannot spend them or see anything else you own.

Private payouts

A cooperative close can pay every party and up to 16 payees in one shielded transaction.

Architecture

Native Zcash contracts

zDLC compiles a conditional agreement into native Zcash transactions: a 2-of-2, one pre-signed payout per outcome, and a time-locked refund. The logic is settled before any money moves.

Transactions
Zcash v6, built through PCZTs; the cooperative close pays the Ironwood shielded pool
Custody
A 2-of-2 between the parties; no contract address and no intermediary
Adaptor signatures
Each pre-signed payout carries the counterparty's ECDSA adaptor signature (secp256k1-zkp, as in rust-dlc), encrypted to the oracles' point for one outcome; only their attestation completes it. This all happens off chain: Zcash sees an ordinary 2-of-2 signature, so it needs no protocol change
Oracle messages
dlcspecs attestations, checked against an independent dlcspecs oracle implementation
Network upgrades
A contract spanning one upgrade has every payout signed for both eras (two adaptor signatures each) and its refund signed for the era it confirms in; tested across NU7 on regtest. One that spans two upgrades is refused
Recovery
With the seed, a wallet finds its recovery records and rebuilds each refund. It needs the record to have been posted (the kit pays for it from the treasury once a contract is signed), a node that can scan from the contract's height, and recovering before the close address is swept by a withdrawal

Not yet

  • Oracles inside hardware enclaves
  • An external security review

Self-custody, pending review

The settlement and refund paths are tested on regtest and testnet but not yet independently audited. Do not use it for real money.

Oracles are trusted for the outcome

They cannot take funds, and two must agree, but colluding oracles can choose which pre-agreed payout unlocks.

Oracle keys

Today's oracles keep their keys in a sealed file, not yet in a hardware enclave.

Demo shortcuts

Both sides of each live demo run on our server so you can play them; a real user runs their own node or the SDK. Arbiter rulings are buttons, and the coins are testnet.

Viewing-key scope

A proof-of-payment oracle can read every payment to the repayment address it watches, including amounts and memos. That address exists for one contract and nothing else.
